# Positioning

## Purpose
Establish Grove's core market framing so all marketing and sales narratives stay coherent and differentiated.

## Scope
Homepage narrative, campaign messaging, outbound positioning, and sales enablement alignment.

## Inputs
- [taxonomy](../../_shared/01-taxonomy.md)
- [quality bar](../../_shared/02-quality-bar.md)
- Repeated objections from sales and support
- Product capability boundaries and proof

## Decision Rules
- Lead with business outcome: reliable execution across company workflows.
- Distinguish from generic AI tooling by emphasizing business context memory, orchestration, and role-specific workflows.
- Explicitly repel low-fit expectations for novelty demos and shallow chat wrappers.
- Keep language concrete enough that a buyer can map claims to an internal process.

## Examples
- Core framing: "Grove turns scattered AI usage into a business operating system your team can actually run."
- Contrast line: "If you want a flashy assistant demo, Grove is not your product. If you want AI tied to how your business actually works, it is."

## Failure Modes
- Category soup: "AI platform for the future of work" with no operational definition.
- Feature-first copy before buyer pain is established.
- Overstating autonomy claims without evidence.
