# Homepage Narrative Spec

## Purpose
Define Grove's homepage message sequence so copy, design, motion, and CTA logic stay strategically coherent.

## Scope
Homepage only. This is the source of truth for section order, message intent, proof placement, and conversion path.

## Primary Audience
- `Tier 1`: operator-led teams (20-200 employees) with visible workflow breakdowns and decision urgency.
- `Tier 2`: team leads with real pain but weaker executive authority.
- Explicitly de-prioritize low-fit visitors seeking generic chatbot demos.

## Narrative Sequence
1. Above-the-fold: qualify and polarize.
- Job: make the right person feel seen in 5 seconds and repel low-fit curiosity traffic.
- Core line: "Grove turns scattered AI usage into a business operating system your team can actually run."
- Contrast line: "Not a flashy chat demo. An execution system tied to how your business works."
- Primary CTA: `See If Grove Fits Your Team`
- Secondary CTA: `Watch 3-Minute Workflow Walkthrough`

2. Problem framing: show execution cost.
- Job: translate "AI chaos" into operational consequences.
- Points to hit:
  - handoffs break
  - process quality depends on who is online
  - pilots never become repeatable systems
- Micro-proof style: quantified pain examples, not abstract trend claims.

3. Mechanism: explain why Grove is different.
- Job: explain product truth with concrete language.
- Required mechanism pillars:
  - business context memory
  - cross-role orchestration
  - workflow-level accountability
- Copy rule: each mechanism statement must map to an example workflow.

4. Role outcomes: operator, team lead, founder.
- Job: let each buyer type find their "so what."
- Format: pain -> intervention -> measurable outcome.
- Example structure:
  - Operator: reduce execution drift across teams.
  - Team lead: standardize quality even when people change.
  - Founder: convert AI experimentation into accountable operating leverage.

5. Proof block: trust before commitment.
- Job: remove "sounds good, prove it" friction.
- Acceptable proof (in priority order):
  - before/after operational metrics
  - named customer process outcomes
  - implementation timeline reality (30/60 day examples)
- Do not use logo walls as primary proof.

6. Fit filter: explicit "not for" language.
- Job: preserve pipeline quality by pre-disqualifying weak-fit demand.
- Required language:
  - not for one-off personal productivity use
  - not for teams without process ownership
  - not for buyers seeking AI novelty demos

7. Conversion block: single clear next step.
- Job: convert high-fit intent with low ambiguity.
- Primary CTA repeated: `See If Grove Fits Your Team`
- Form entry promise: "10-minute fit assessment. You get a clear yes/no and why."
- Capture fields should support qualification, not just contact collection.

## CTA Architecture
- Top nav CTA: `See If Grove Fits Your Team`
- Mid-page CTA: `See Workflow Examples`
- Bottom-page CTA: `Request Qualified Demo`
- Rule: each CTA must imply buyer intent depth. Avoid duplicate CTAs with different labels for the same action.

## Visual and Motion Guidance
- Motion must reinforce sequence:
  - problem section: controlled friction, not decorative movement
  - mechanism section: progressive reveal by workflow step
  - proof section: stable and literal, no theatrical transitions
- Avoid ornamental animation that competes with copy comprehension.

## Acceptance Criteria
- A new visitor can answer in under 20 seconds:
  - what Grove is
  - who it is for
  - why it is different
  - what to do next
- Low-fit visitors are explicitly filtered before form submission.
- Every major claim has adjacent proof or a clear evidence placeholder.

## Dependencies
- Sales: top objections and win/loss language
- CS: onboarding failure patterns and activation milestones
- Product/Engineering: current demonstrable capability boundaries
